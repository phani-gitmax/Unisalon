Winter is a good time to change your hair. The festive and wedding season is on, the harsh summer sun is behind us, and a fresh colour or cut is easier to look after when you're not sweating through the day.

The good news for Indian hair: this year's trends are softer, warmer and built around natural dark bases, not platinum blonde. Here's what's trending, who it suits and how to ask for it.

## The big idea: soft dimension, low upkeep

L'Oréal Paris India's 2026 trend guide sums up the mood: **soft dimension over harsh contrast**, colours chosen for your skin undertone, and a focus on hair health with gentler processing. It lists extreme platinum, chunky high-contrast highlights and flat, one-tone colour among the looks that are fading out.

In practice, that means colour that grows out gracefully, so you don't need a touch-up every few weeks.

## 1. Bronde blends

If one shade defines 2026 for Indian hair, it's **bronde**, a blend of brown and blonde. L'Oréal Professionnel declared Bronde Blends its hair colour trend of 2026, saying women across India are increasingly drawn to brown-based shades that feel natural. Harper's Bazaar India covered the launch with Samantha Ruth Prabhu, describing tones from honey and caramel to teddy and ash as "tailored beautifully to Indian skin tones".

- **Suits:** most Indian skin tones. Warm honey and caramel flatter wheatish and golden undertones; ashier, mushroom-type browns work better on cooler undertones.
- **How to ask:** bronde is created with placement techniques such as balayage, babylights or ombré highlights rather than an all-over colour. Ask for soft, blended pieces, not stripes.

## 2. Mocha, chocolate and "teddy" brunettes

Rich browns are everywhere. L'Oréal Paris India names **soft mocha brunette** and **melted caramel** (a mocha base melting into caramel and chestnut) among the biggest colours of 2026. For winter, The Zoe Report's colourists recommend "teddy brown", a warm brown with hints of caramel and cocoa, and Harper's Bazaar India reports stylists seeing a move to rich chocolate browns, partly because they're gentler on hair than repeated bleaching.

- **Suits:** almost everyone, and it's ideal if you want a change without going much lighter than your natural shade.
- **Bonus:** less regrowth contrast, so touch-ups can wait longer.

## 3. Wine, plum and muted copper

For something bolder, **wine red** was the winter pick of all three experts The Zoe Report spoke to, who said it is especially striking on deeper skin tones and natural brunettes. L'Oréal Paris India also lists **muted copper** and **rose auburn** as 2026's softer, more wearable reds.

- **Good to know:** reds fade faster. The Zoe Report's experts suggest refreshing regrowth and gloss every four to six weeks, and using sulfate-free, colour-protecting products at home.

## 4. Low-maintenance options

Not ready for a full colour? The L'Oréal Paris India guide highlights:

- **Root-shadowed colour:** a slightly deeper root that blends with new growth
- **Face-framing highlights:** brightness around the face without colouring your whole head
- **Glosses:** shine and tone with less commitment

## Haircuts to pair with your colour

Colour shows best on a good cut. Elle India's 2026 haircut guide picks several styles that work well for Indian hair:

- **Waterfall layers** for long hair, which Elle says suit Indian hair densities especially well
- **The off-duty lob**, collarbone length with soft layers, which handles humidity better than very long styles
- **Invisible layers** that remove weight without visible steps, so hair falls better when air-dried
- **Curtain bangs**, which a stylist quoted by Harper's Bazaar India calls forgiving and soft enough for most face shapes

## Winter care for coloured hair in Hyderabad

Cooler, drier winter air and hard borewell water can leave coloured hair dull and rough. A few simple habits help:

- Wash with a gentle, colour-safe shampoo, and don't wash more than you need to.
- Always condition, and use heat protection before ironing or tongs.
