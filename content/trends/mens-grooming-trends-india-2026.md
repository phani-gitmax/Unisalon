Men's grooming in India has moved well beyond a quick trim and a shave. More men now have a skincare routine, care about their beard shape and are happy to try a new cut. Here's what the numbers say, and the trends worth knowing this year.

## The numbers: a growing market

Two recent market reports put India's men's grooming market at around **USD 2.6 billion in 2026**:

- **IMARC Group** estimates the India male grooming products market grew from USD 2.45 billion in 2025 to USD 2.62 billion in 2026, and projects USD 4.46 billion by 2034 (a 6.66% annual growth rate). It names the **rapid expansion of unisex and male salons** as one of the main growth drivers, alongside rising incomes, wellness awareness and social media.
- **MarkNtel Advisors** values the market at USD 2.47 billion in 2025 and USD 2.64 billion in 2026, growing about 7.2% a year to USD 4.01 billion by 2032. It notes the category is moving beyond shaving into beard care, skincare, haircare and fragrance.

Both reports measure grooming *products*, not salon services. But they point the same way: Indian men are spending more time and money on how they look.

## Trend 1: The textured crop and the fade

The two cuts we'd call the safest bets for 2026 are the **fade** and the **textured crop**. Esquire India's March 2026 guide calls the fade a classic that "is always going to remain in style", in low, mid or high versions. It says the textured crop, short on the sides and a little longer on top, is popular because it doesn't need much maintenance and looks great on thick or wavy hair.

**Good for:** office-goers and students who want a sharp look with five minutes of styling. A matte clay or paste with light hold is enough.

## Trend 2: The modern mullet

Yes, really. In February 2026, Esquire India declared the mullet "back to being a respectable hairstyle", now cleaned up into softer versions: baby mullets, cropped mullets and faded mullets. The one rule is that the back stays the longest part.

**How to ask for it:** bring reference photos, and explain two things: how tight you want the sides, and how much length you're comfortable keeping at the back. Esquire also advises against cutting one yourself. Mullets rely on careful layering, so this is a cut for a stylist who is comfortable with scissor work.

## Trend 3: Longer hair and natural waves

The same Esquire India guide says long, natural waves are back in trend, along with the low-effort **buzz cut** at the opposite end. Wearing your natural texture instead of fighting it is the common thread.

For longer hair, conditioning matters more, especially through Hyderabad's dry winter months and with hard water. A [L'Oréal hair spa](/services/hair-spa-in-manikonda) (₹800 for men, ₹1,100 with concentrate) every few weeks keeps lengths soft.

## Trend 4: Beard care becomes routine

Facial hair is common among Indian men, and research firm Mintel's *India Men's Grooming Market Report 2026* highlights beard care as a growth area, including concerns such as patchy growth among Gen Z and grey hair in the beard. The takeaway for you: a beard looks best when it's shaped and cared for, not just left to grow.

- Get the neckline and cheek lines shaped regularly.
- Wash and condition your beard, not just your hair.
- Moisturise the skin underneath, especially if it gets dry and itchy in winter.
